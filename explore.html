Sun & light

Which way does the sun fall on your flat?

East, west or north decides your morning light, your evening heat, and how your balcony feels. These tools plot the real sun path over the site — pick a date and time and watch the shadows move. Drag the pin onto your exact tower for a precise read.

☀️ Summer sun (21 Jun)

Longest day — the sun rises far to the north-east and sets north-west. See how high it climbs and how short the shadows get at noon.

🌤️ Equinox (21 Mar)

Sun rises due east, sets due west — the balanced day. A fair "average" read of how light crosses your flat.

🌥️ Winter sun (21 Dec)

Shortest day — the sun stays low and to the south, throwing long shadows. This is when lower floors feel the most overshadowing from nearby towers.

Views & facing

What each side looks out on.

Gathered from owners in the group — practical, lived observations rather than brochure copy. Treat these as owner notes to verify against your own cost sheet and a site visit, not official commitments.

🌅 East vs west

East-facing flats are the traditional favourite; owners note west-facing units get strong morning sun in the west balcony, roughly 10–11am to ~6pm. It comes down to whether you prefer morning light or a bright evening balcony.

🌊 Lake & greenery

Owners report Towers 8 & 9 get the lake view, while Tower 10 gets both agricultural greenery and the lake. Higher floors, corner units and private-corridor flats are the ones most prized for view.

🏬 Mall & lower floors

The mall's height is said to sit in front of the lower ~10 floors of Towers 1, 2 and 3 — worth checking sightlines and light for lower units there against the 3D tour and the shadow tools above.

🚪 Door-open privacy

A point some owners raised: on certain north-facing units the opposite flat's utility/wash area can be in the sightline when the main door opens (noted around a Tower 11 corner). A closed shaft wall covers it in many stacks — check your specific unit's plan.
